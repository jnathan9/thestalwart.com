Strongest signals in your text

From the same simpler model.
Verdict model. ModernBERT-base, fine-tuned on 25,311 text chunks. Trained on raw, markdown-stripped and topic-masked copies of every document so it can't win by reading formatting or subject matter. Held-out accuracy 96.4% in-genre; 79-90% on genres it never trained on.
Normalization. Text is unicode-normalized first. Without that the task is trivial and meaningless — one family emits curly apostrophes 77% of the time and the other emits straight ones 88% of the time, which is an encoding artifact, not a voice.
Reading the score. A similarity score, not a detector. 100 means the writing sits squarely in Claude's register, 0 squarely in GPT's. A high score doesn't mean a model wrote it — it means whoever did writes more like Claude than like Chat.
Short text is hard. Under ~60 words there genuinely isn't much voice to read, and the score gets unreliable. The app tells you when that applies.
