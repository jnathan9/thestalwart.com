What happens when you upload

Contributions are open.

  1. Your browser prepares the sample. It removes reposts, link-only posts, and very short posts, reconstructs self-reply threads, and spreads 10,000 words across the latest three years. You review the exact text before submitting.
  2. The sample is processed privately. The same fixed model describes what you return to, what you believe, and how you explain what causes what. The sample is deleted after the profile passes its quality check.
  3. Your name is added without a label. The descriptions become map coordinates, but they are never shown as an explanation of where you landed.

There is no sign-in, ever. The site will not ask you for a password, and there is nothing to log into, so it never holds a credential of yours that could later leak. The other half of that is worth saying out loud: nothing checks whose archive it was. No identity check is performed at any point, and contributing is not treated as proof of anything.

Your complete archive never leaves your device. The reviewed sample is stored privately while it is processed and deleted after a successful profile and receipt have been written. The model-written descriptions and map coordinates remain public until you ask to be removed.

Some of the profiles on the map above were seeded rather than contributed, and are drawn as hollow circles. Those people uploaded nothing. Their posts were taken from a public archive by whoever built this page, and they did not choose to be here. They are marked differently for that reason and for no other.

The seeded profiles come from the Community Archive, where those people published their whole posting history in the open for anyone to read and analyse. That is what it is there for. Being named on someone else's map of who resembles whom, and described on it in prose you did not write, is a further step, and nobody on this page agreed to it. So if you are on this map and would rather not be, say so and you will be removed. No reason needed and none will be asked for.

How the map is built

The archive is opened and cut down in the contributor's browser. Retweets, link-only posts, and posts under five words are removed. Self-reply threads are reconstructed. A fixed 10,000-word budget is sampled across the most recent three years so verbosity and one unusually active month do not decide the map.

The reviewed sample is handed to the same fixed language model under the same instructions for everyone. It writes three descriptions:

  • what you keep returning to
  • what you actually think
  • how you think the world works — what causes what

Each description is then turned into a long list of numbers. That conversion is called an embedding. The arrangement is built so that two pieces of writing that say similar things come out with similar numbers. That is the entire trick: similar writing, similar numbers. Once you have numbers, closeness is just arithmetic.

After extraction and a quality check, the sample is deleted. What remains is the three descriptions, their numerical representations, a consent receipt without posts, and the chosen public name.

People whose numbers land close together are neighbours. You see their names, their rank, and a cohort-relative closeness percentile, and never a reason for any of it. The descriptions those numbers were made from are retained to make the map, not displayed as labels or attached to a rank as an explanation.

The picture is worked out twice: once from recurring subjects, and once from the equal-weight combination of positions and causal explanations. Moving the dial blends those two settled arrangements, so points glide rather than jump.

What has been measured

Two non-overlapping 10,000-word samples of five people were independently placed against the same eleven-person reference map. Both lenses retained 73% of their top-three neighbors. Attention retained the exact top neighbor 60% of the time; Ideology retained it in all five cases. This is still a small cohort, so the arrangement above remains visibly marked provisional.
